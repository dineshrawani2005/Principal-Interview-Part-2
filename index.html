KVS / NVS Principal Interview-PART-2

VP/PGTCandidate • 23 High-Value Chapters • Questions + Model Answers + Situational Preparation
Use current official rules/notifications for time-sensitive or rule-specific answers.
Developed by:Dinesh Kumar Ram, PGT(CS)

1. NEP 2020 (4 questions)
Q1. What is the central vision of NEP 2020?
Model Answer: To transform education into a holistic, flexible, multidisciplinary, inclusive and competency-oriented system, with strong emphasis on foundational learning, critical thinking, creativity, values, technology and lifelong learning.
Q2. As a Principal, how will you implement NEP 2020?
Model Answer: I would translate policy into a school improvement plan covering foundational learning, competency-based pedagogy, experiential learning, vocational exposure, inclusive education, technology integration, teacher development and holistic assessment.
Q3. How can technology support NEP 2020?
Model Answer: Technology can support personalised learning, digital resources, assessment, teacher collaboration, data analysis, accessibility and blended learning. It should remain a means to improve learning, not an end in itself.
Q4. What is the 5+3+3+4 structure?
Model Answer: It reorganises school education into Foundational, Preparatory, Middle and Secondary stages, aligned with children's developmental needs and appropriate pedagogy.
2. NCF-SE (3 questions)
Q1. What is NCF-SE 2023?
Model Answer: The National Curriculum Framework for School Education 2023 provides curricular and pedagogical guidance aligned with NEP 2020, focusing on holistic development, competency-based learning, multidisciplinary learning, appropriate pedagogy and assessment.
Q2. How will you implement NCF-SE in your school?
Model Answer: Through curriculum planning, competency-focused lesson plans, experiential activities, appropriate assessment, integration of subjects and regular teacher capacity building.
Q3. What change does competency-based curriculum require from teachers?
Model Answer: Teachers need to move from primarily content transmission to designing learning experiences in which students apply concepts, reason, communicate, create and solve authentic problems.
3. FLN (3 questions)
Q1. What is Foundational Literacy and Numeracy?
Model Answer: FLN means ensuring that children acquire essential reading, writing, comprehension and numeracy abilities in the early years so that they can learn effectively in later grades.
Q2. What will you do if foundational learning is weak?
Model Answer: Use baseline assessment, identify specific gaps, provide targeted interventions, involve parents, monitor progress frequently and support teachers with suitable resources and training.
Q3. How will a Principal monitor FLN?
Model Answer: Through periodic evidence of student competencies, classroom observation, assessment data, teacher review meetings and intervention tracking—not merely through activity completion.
4. Competency-based Learning (3 questions)
Q1. What is competency-based learning?
Model Answer: It focuses on what learners can understand, apply, analyse, create and demonstrate rather than only what they can recall.
Q2. How will you promote competency-based learning?
Model Answer: Use real-life problems, case studies, projects, experiments, discussions, application-oriented questions, rubrics and formative feedback.
Q3. How will you change examinations?
Model Answer: Encourage questions requiring application, analysis, reasoning and problem-solving while maintaining alignment with the prescribed curriculum and examination framework.
5. Experiential Learning (3 questions)
Q1. What is experiential learning?
Model Answer: Learning through meaningful experiences followed by reflection, application and feedback. Examples include projects, experiments, field work, simulations and community-based activities.
Q2. How can a Principal promote it?
Model Answer: Provide planning time, encourage interdisciplinary projects, use laboratories and local resources, organise purposeful field experiences and review whether activities actually produce learning outcomes.
Q3. Give a CS example.
Model Answer: Students can design a simple data-based solution to a real school problem, analyse the data, develop a prototype and present their findings.
6. Inclusive Education (3 questions)
Q1. What is inclusive education?
Model Answer: An approach in which diverse learners learn together with appropriate accessibility, support, reasonable accommodation and participation.
Q2. How will you make a school inclusive?
Model Answer: Audit physical and digital accessibility, support children with disabilities, sensitise staff, provide accommodations, strengthen peer support and work with parents and relevant professionals.
Q3. What if a teacher feels inclusion increases workload?
Model Answer: Listen to the concern, identify practical barriers, provide support and training, simplify processes and demonstrate workable classroom strategies.
7. POCSO (3 questions)
Q1. What should a Principal do when a POCSO-related allegation is reported?
Model Answer: Ensure immediate child safety, maintain a child-sensitive approach, avoid repeated or leading questioning, follow the statutory reporting and response process, preserve relevant information and maintain confidentiality.
Q2. What if the accused is another student?
Model Answer: Protect all children, avoid labelling or humiliating the accused child, and ensure the matter is handled through the legally prescribed child-sensitive procedure.
Q3. How can a school prevent POCSO incidents?
Model Answer: Regular awareness, safe infrastructure, staff sensitisation, supervision, visitor controls, reporting mechanisms, age-appropriate safety education and prompt action on concerns.
8. Child Safety (3 questions)
Q1. What are the key elements of school child safety?
Model Answer: Safe infrastructure, supervision, emergency preparedness, visitor management, transport safety where applicable, cyber safety, grievance mechanisms and trained staff.
Q2. What if a parent becomes abusive in front of students?
Model Answer: Protect students from the confrontation, remain calm, move the discussion to a private setting, hear the grievance, set respectful boundaries and follow the prescribed process if misconduct continues.
Q3. What if CCTV footage of a student is circulating online?
Model Answer: Protect the child's privacy, restrict further dissemination, preserve relevant evidence, follow authorised reporting procedures and avoid publicly identifying the child.
9. UDISE+ (3 questions)
Q1. What is UDISE+?
Model Answer: UDISE+ is the national school education data system used for collecting and managing school education data for planning, monitoring and policy purposes.
Q2. Why is data accuracy important?
Model Answer: School planning, resource allocation, monitoring and policy decisions depend on reliable data. A Principal should establish verification and accountability before submission.
Q3. What will you do if school data contains errors?
Model Answer: Identify the source, verify against original records, correct through the authorised workflow, document the correction and strengthen verification before future submission.
10. APAAR (3 questions)
Q1. What is APAAR?
Model Answer: APAAR stands for Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. It is intended to provide students with a persistent academic identity/account framework under the applicable government system.
Q2. What problems may occur during APAAR generation?
Model Answer: Data mismatch, demographic discrepancies, consent or verification issues, technical problems and duplicate records may occur. The school should resolve them through the prescribed workflow.
Q3. What is the Principal's responsibility?
Model Answer: Ensure accurate student records, appropriate consent/verification procedures, awareness among parents and staff, confidentiality and timely resolution of genuine issues.
11. DIKSHA (3 questions)
Q1. What is DIKSHA?
Model Answer: DIKSHA is a national digital infrastructure/platform for school education resources, teacher learning and related educational initiatives.
Q2. How will you use DIKSHA in school?
Model Answer: Encourage teachers to use relevant digital resources, QR-linked content and professional-learning resources, while ensuring that technology supports—not replaces—effective pedagogy.
Q3. How will you monitor digital resource use?
Model Answer: Through lesson planning, classroom observation, teacher sharing and evidence of student learning rather than simply counting logins or activities.
12. AI Literacy (3 questions)
Q1. How should AI be used in schools?
Model Answer: For appropriate educational support such as idea generation, differentiation, resource preparation, analysis and accessibility, with teacher verification, privacy protection and human oversight.
Q2. What are risks of AI in education?
Model Answer: Inaccurate outputs, bias, privacy risks, overdependence, plagiarism and inappropriate content. Schools need clear guidance and responsible-use practices.
Q3. How will you promote AI literacy?
Model Answer: Teach students to understand AI concepts, verify outputs, recognise bias, protect personal data, use AI ethically and retain human judgement.
13. Cyber Safety (3 questions)
Q1. What is cyber safety in a school?
Model Answer: Protecting students, staff, devices, accounts and information from phishing, malware, unauthorised access, privacy breaches and harmful online behaviour.
Q2. What basic controls should a school have?
Model Answer: Strong passwords, multi-factor authentication where available, controlled access, regular updates, backups, awareness training, secure sharing and incident-response procedures.
Q3. A teacher clicks a phishing link. What will you do?
Model Answer: Contain the incident promptly, secure the account/device, change credentials as required, report through the appropriate IT process, preserve relevant information and use the incident for awareness training.
14. Data-driven Decisions (3 questions)
Q1. What is data-driven school leadership?
Model Answer: Using reliable evidence—attendance, learning outcomes, participation, infrastructure and other relevant indicators—to identify problems, plan interventions and monitor improvement.
Q2. How will you avoid misuse of data?
Model Answer: Use accurate, relevant and proportionate data, protect confidentiality, avoid labelling students unfairly and combine quantitative evidence with professional judgement.
Q3. Give a school example.
Model Answer: Analyse subject-wise learning gaps, identify priority competencies, plan remediation, assign responsibility and review progress after a defined period.
15. GFR (3 questions)
Q1. What is GFR?
Model Answer: General Financial Rules provide the framework for financial management and government expenditure, including principles and procedures relating to procurement, expenditure, accountability and public resources.
Q2. What is your principle for handling school funds?
Model Answer: Economy, efficiency, transparency, accountability, proper authority, documentation and compliance with applicable rules.
Q3. What if funds are insufficient?
Model Answer: Prioritise essential expenditure, review commitments, use permissible options within rules and avoid non-essential spending. Never compromise safety or financial compliance.
16. GeM (3 questions)
Q1. What is GeM?
Model Answer: Government e-Marketplace is the government's online procurement platform for eligible goods and services, subject to applicable procurement rules.
Q2. What is the Principal's role in procurement?
Model Answer: Ensure genuine requirement, proper approval, specification, procurement procedure, receipt and verification, documentation and accountability.
Q3. What if a supplier offers an informal favour?
Model Answer: Decline it and maintain transparency and integrity. Procurement decisions must be based on official requirements and applicable rules.
17. CRAC (3 questions)
Q1. What is CRAC?
Model Answer: CRAC generally refers to the Consignee Receipt and Acceptance Certificate in the government procurement workflow, recording receipt and acceptance as applicable.
Q2. Why is CRAC important?
Model Answer: It provides documentary evidence that supplied goods/services were received and accepted according to the applicable procurement process.
Q3. What should a Principal ensure?
Model Answer: Verification of quantity, quality/specification and supporting documentation before authorised acceptance.
18. Team Management (3 questions)
Q1. How will you build an effective school team?
Model Answer: Set a shared vision, define responsibilities, communicate clearly, delegate appropriately, recognise good work, support professional development and review outcomes.
Q2. How will you motivate teachers?
Model Answer: Recognition, meaningful responsibility, professional autonomy with accountability, collaborative planning, capacity building and a respectful work environment.
Q3. How do you handle an underperforming team member?
Model Answer: Understand the reason, clarify expectations, provide support and a reasonable improvement plan, monitor progress and use the prescribed process when necessary.
19. Conflict Resolution (3 questions)
Q1. Two teachers have a conflict. What will you do?
Model Answer: Hear both sides separately, establish facts, focus on the school's interest, facilitate professional resolution, document necessary decisions and monitor the situation.
Q2. A senior teacher disagrees with your decision.
Model Answer: Listen respectfully, explain the rationale, examine whether the concern has merit and maintain the legitimate decision if required, using due process.
Q3. What is your conflict-management principle?
Model Answer: Respect people, separate the person from the issue, rely on facts and rules, communicate clearly and seek a workable institutional solution.
20. PM SHRI (3 questions)
Q1. What is PM SHRI?
Model Answer: PM SHRI schools are intended to showcase implementation of NEP 2020 through quality, inclusive and equitable education, innovative pedagogy, improved infrastructure and a positive learning environment.
Q2. How will you make PM SHRI meaningful?
Model Answer: Align activities with student needs and measurable learning outcomes, involve teachers and students, use resources sustainably and monitor impact rather than focusing only on activities.
Q3. How can technology support PM SHRI?
Model Answer: Digital resources, smart learning environments, data use, innovation, coding/AI literacy and technology-enabled experiential learning can support school transformation.
21. Assessment (4 questions)
Q1. What is formative assessment?
Model Answer: Assessment conducted during learning to identify progress and gaps and provide feedback that improves subsequent learning.
Q2. Portfolio vs anecdotal record?
Model Answer: A portfolio is a purposeful collection of student work showing progress; an anecdotal record is a brief factual record of a significant observed behaviour or event.
Q3. How will you make assessment holistic?
Model Answer: Combine classroom evidence, projects, performance, portfolios, observation, competency-based tasks and appropriate formal assessment while maintaining the prescribed framework.
Q4. How will you improve board results?
Model Answer: Analyse learning gaps, provide targeted remediation, strengthen attendance and teaching quality, monitor progress and support students without reducing education to marks alone.
22. School Safety (3 questions)
Q1. What is a comprehensive school safety plan?
Model Answer: A coordinated plan covering physical safety, fire and disaster preparedness, child protection, health, sanitation, visitor management, transport where applicable, cyber safety and emergency communication.
Q2. Two emergencies occur simultaneously. What will you do?
Model Answer: Prioritise threats to life and safety, activate emergency protocols, delegate one response to a competent team, maintain communication, account for students/staff and review the incident afterward.
Q3. How will you prepare students and staff for emergencies?
Model Answer: Regular drills, clear roles, evacuation routes, emergency contacts, first-aid readiness, orientation and periodic review of the safety plan.
Principal Interview Answer Formula: Situation → Rule/Principle → Immediate Action → Team/Stakeholders → Documentation → Monitoring → Improvement.
